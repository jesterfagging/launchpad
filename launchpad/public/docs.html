Launch a coin

  1. Connect your wallet. Connect a Solana wallet and approve the sign-in message. Your first sign-in creates a passwordless account with a randomized username. Adding a profile picture or bio is optional.
  2. Build your launch. Add a name, ticker, image, and description. Choose a launch venue (Pump.fun, Raydium or Meteora), an available paired asset, and a reward or launch option where supported. Leave Website blank to use this coin’s public Launchpad page as its metadata website, or enter your own project website.
  3. Review the numbers. Check the wallet, quote asset, valuation, creator fee, transfer tax, liquidity allocation, and required SOL before approving.
  4. Approve and follow progress. The launch can require more than one step. Keep the saved launch open until its transactions are confirmed.

Supported pairs can include crypto assets, tokenized stocks and ETFs, and pre-IPO assets. Availability depends on the token, its issuer, and a compatible live configuration for the selected launch mode. Appearing in an asset list does not guarantee that a launch or swap is available at that moment.

Your new coin is a separate token. Pairing it with a stock token does not make your coin a share in that company or give it the issuer's redemption rights.

Pump.fun launches

Choose Pump.fun to create a native Pump.fun coin through Launchpad. It uses Pump.fun’s bonding curve for the pair you choose, trades on Pump.fun and moves to PumpSwap when the curve completes. Pump.fun’s own fees, curve parameters and graduation rules apply; Launchpad adds no trade fee of its own. Pairs are SOL and the other quote assets Pump.fun admits for new coins; see Pairs below.

  1. Review a quote. Pick a pair, a launch option and an optional dev buy. You enter the dev buy in SOL. The quote lists it and, on another pair, about how much of that pair’s asset it buys. It also lists the launch costs (about 0.014 SOL on SOL and about 0.024 SOL on another pair, within a budget whose unused part comes back after launch), an operating reserve for the option’s Launchpad wallets and, for the two strategies, optional seed capital. A quote is valid for 10 minutes.
  2. Fund it with one transfer. Your wallet signs one SOL transfer to a Launchpad launch wallet created only for this coin. It covers the dev buy, the launch costs and the reserves, on every pair; you never need the pair’s asset. The transaction carries a launch memo, and Launchpad checks the signed bytes before submitting them. Nothing else needs your signature.
  3. Launchpad creates the coin in one transaction. After the transfer finalizes, the launch wallet creates the coin, sets Pump.fun’s native creator-fee sharing to the recipients of your option, revokes the sharing admin and makes your dev buy, all in one transaction, so the dev buy is always the first buy. On SOL the dev-buy tokens reach your funding wallet in that same transaction. On another pair Launchpad first buys the pair asset with your dev-buy SOL and creates the pair’s token accounts the coin uses, and your tokens reach your wallet in a second transaction a few seconds after the coin is created. Every transaction is simulated against its exact effects before it is signed, and saved before it is sent.
  4. Unused funds return to you. The unused part of the launch-cost budget is refunded in SOL to your funding wallet. On another pair, any of the pair’s asset left over after the dev buy is sent there too. If the coin cannot be created, the funding is refunded instead, in SOL: if Launchpad had already bought the pair asset, it sells it back to SOL first (and returns the asset itself only if it cannot). Network fees, rent for accounts that belong to the coin’s pair and fee recipients, and the swap’s price movement are not refundable.

The fee-sharing recipients are fixed when the coin is created and the sharing admin is revoked, so nobody, including Launchpad, can redirect them later. Every Pump.fun option includes a platform share of the coin’s creator fees for Launchpad; the shares are listed under Revenue. Creator fees are distributed by Pump.fun’s permissionless distribution instruction, which anyone can call. Launchpad records each distribution once, from its finalized transaction.

Pairs

Pump.fun admits a set of quote assets for new coins: SOL, the asset it lists itself (such as USDC) and a list of custom pairs it approves (for example tokenized stocks). Launchpad reads that list live from Pump.fun’s program when you open the pair picker and, once this site opens pairs other than SOL, offers every pair Launchpad can handle exactly. A pair stays listed with the reason when Launchpad cannot use it yet, for example when its token runs a transfer hook program, charges a transfer fee or creates frozen accounts. If Pump.fun stops admitting a pair before your coin is created, the launch is refunded.

  • Your dev buy is paid in SOL. Once your funding settles, Launchpad buys the pair asset with exactly that SOL through a verified swap, with a 2% slippage limit and a 2% price-impact limit, and the dev buy spends all of it. The quote shows about how much the swap buys, the least you get (what the swap guarantees, less 5% for the price to move before it runs) and the tokens that buys. A launch keeps the limits it was quoted with. Launchpad only sends the swap while it still guarantees that least amount. A dev buy needs a pair Launchpad can buy with SOL; on other pairs you can launch without one. If the swap cannot be made within its limits after a few tries, the launch is refunded in SOL and nothing is converted. If the coin is not created after the swap, Launchpad sells the pair asset back to SOL for your refund. The dev-buy limit is the SOL limit, also scaled to the pair by Pump.fun’s starting curve reserves for that pair.
  • Creator fees arrive in the pair’s asset. With the Creator option your share is paid to your funding wallet in that asset. With Holders, holder rewards are paid in that asset too: Launchpad sells only a small part of them for the token accounts new recipients need and the payout’s network fees. Launchpad converts its platform share and a Gotchi share to SOL through a verified swap before routing them. A conversion swaps at market prices with a 1% slippage limit and waits, and retries, when no route is available.
  • Holders, Diamond Hands and Gotchi need a pair Launchpad can sell for SOL. Creator works on every pair Launchpad can handle. AI Agent, Market Maker, Liquidity Manager, Earned and Insured pair with SOL only, because their wallets trade, escrow and settle in SOL.
  • Custom pairs can carry a creator fee you choose. Pump.fun lets coins on its custom pairs set their own creator fee, up to a limit it publishes and can change. You can keep Pump.fun’s standard fee or choose a rate up to that limit; traders pay it, it is shared as your option describes, and Launchpad does not change it after creation.
  • Issuer controls stay with the issuer. Some pair tokens, such as tokenized stocks, let their issuer pause transfers or move tokens, and may be restricted in some jurisdictions. Those rules apply to the pair asset in your wallet, in the coin’s pool and in Launchpad’s wallets; a paused token delays dev-buy funding, refunds and conversions until it moves again.

Diamond Hands, Earned and Insured

Three launch options turn the coin’s creator-fee share into a rule with your numbers. The numbers are chosen on the launch page, written on the launch when the coin is created and never change afterwards; the coin page shows the rule in one sentence with your numbers, its state and every payout. Each option includes the same platform share for Launchpad as Holders. Nothing in them promises a price, a graduation or a return.

  • Diamond Hands is Holders with a memory. Rewards go to the top 100 holders by minimum balance over the last hour whenever the holders’ share reaches about $100, as for Holders, except that a wallet whose balance dropped since its last payout is skipped for that payout and its streak starts over. Every consecutive payout a wallet holds through adds the bonus per payout held (0–50%, default 10%) to its share, up to the bonus cap (0–400%, default 100%, so a share can at most double); a cap of 0% counts streaks without changing shares. It takes the pairs Holders takes and pays in the pair’s asset; a reward basket is a Holders option only.
  • Earned puts your creator fees in escrow. Pump.fun pays your share to a Launchpad escrow wallet made for the coin. Probation (7–90 days, default 30) runs from the coin’s creation; once a day Launchpad records the coin’s market cap, and the first record, a day after launch, is the baseline. A record below the floor (10–200% of the baseline, default 50%) fails the coin: the escrow and every later fee are paid to holders as holder rewards, and the coin page says the coin failed its probation. Surviving the probation releases the escrow to the wallet you funded the launch from, and every later fee is paid to you. Earned pairs with SOL only.
  • Insured keeps a refund pot. Pump.fun pays your share to a Launchpad wallet made for the coin; Launchpad keeps the coverage (10–100%, default 50%) of every receipt as the pot and pays you the rest at once. If the coin has not graduated within the deadline (7–90 days, default 30, checked every hour), the pot refunds holders — paid like holder rewards, to the top 100 holders by minimum balance over the last hour — and from then on the coverage share keeps paying them while the rest is still yours. If it graduates in time, the pot is released to you or paid to holders as a bonus, as you chose at launch, and every later fee is paid to you. Insured pairs with SOL only.

Because the escrow and the pot are Launchpad-managed wallets created for the coin, the fee-sharing recipient is fixed at creation like every other option and cannot be redirected afterwards, by Launchpad included. Payouts to holders wait for the same minimums as Holders rewards (a share worth less than 0.001 SOL is dropped and spread among the others). The daily market-cap records and the deadline checks are Launchpad’s own readings of the coin’s bonding curve, kept on the coin’s record and shown on its page.

Market Maker and Liquidity Manager

Both strategies run only from a Launchpad-managed wallet created for that coin. That wallet is never the wallet that creates the coin, so strategy trades are not dev sells on the chart. Creator fees and any seed capital you add stay with the coin’s strategy: they are not a deposit, you cannot withdraw them, and they carry no ownership or reward claim. Capital added from fees or seed is tracked as contributed capital and is never reported as profit.

Market Maker limits: it trades only when its mix drifts more than 10 percentage points from its target, moves at most 10% of its value per trade, waits at least 2 minutes between trades and 10 minutes before reversing direction, does not reverse direction unless the price has moved at least 1% from its own last fill, trades at most twice its value per UTC day and spends at most 2% of its value on network fees per day. Each trade uses 1% slippage and a 2% price-impact limit. It pauses itself if its value falls 20% below its contributed capital.

Liquidity Manager limits: it adds liquidity only to the canonical PumpSwap pool after a verified graduation, in deposits of at least 0.1 SOL, after swapping just enough SOL for the matching token side. LP tokens stay in the strategy wallet. Deposits and swaps use 1% slippage and a 2% price-impact limit. It pauses itself if its value falls 50% below its contributed capital.

Trading and liquidity can lose value. Launchpad can pause, resume or retire a strategy and reports its status, capital, value and every trade on the coin page. After retiring a strategy, Launchpad can return the SOL in its wallet to the wallet that funded the launch (a small reserve stays so the coin’s fee sharing keeps working); its tokens and liquidity stay in the strategy wallet. Nothing here promises price support, volume, liquidity or profit.

Robinhood Chain launches

Choose Robinhood in the Chain step to create a coin on Robinhood Chain through Launchpad’s own launch contracts. Every coin is a Uniswap v4 pool from its first block against the pair you choose — ETH, USDG, a tokenised stock or ETF from Robinhood’s stock tokens (NVDA, AAPL, SPY…) or a token Launchpad added — so it trades on Launchpad, in the Uniswap app, on GMGN, DexScreener and from any wallet from the moment it exists. The whole supply is liquidity: the part that sells on the way to graduation sits in a position between the start and graduation prices (that position is the bonding curve — same prices, same raise as Launchpad’s Solana curves), the rest in a position above graduation, both held by a locker contract with no withdraw function and no owner. Nothing can ever remove the liquidity. The pair row works as on Solana: ETH, USDG and Custom, which opens the catalogue of every pair Launchpad offers with its live price. Paste any Robinhood Chain token address there to check it: Launchpad reads the token on the chain and tells you whether it is available, not open on this site yet, or a compatible token Launchpad has not added (a Uniswap pool must price it against USDG or ETH) — in which case the operator is notified of your request. Two starts are offered, the same as Solana’s: a $4,000 start (a steep curve that sells 78% of the supply on the way to the $50,000 graduation) and a $20,000 start (61% sold on the way). Dollar values move with the pair asset.

  1. Connect an EVM wallet. MetaMask, Rabby or any EVM wallet; the launch dialog adds Robinhood Chain to it when it is missing. That wallet is the coin’s creator and receives your first buy. You can link the EVM wallet you use to your Launchpad account from Edit profile → Connect EVM wallet (your wallet signs a short message; nothing is spent and your sign-in does not change): the launch and coin pages then name it first, and your Robinhood coins, trades and rewards belong to your account.
  2. Review a quote. It lists the launch fee, your first buy with about how many tokens it buys, a gas budget whose unused part comes back, and, for an option that runs from a Launchpad wallet, a little ETH for that wallet’s gas. A quote is valid for 10 minutes.
  3. Fund it with one transfer. Your wallet sends the total to a Launchpad launch wallet made only for this coin (plus the pair asset, when your first buy is in a stock or USDG). Launchpad confirms the transfer, creates the coin, its pool and the locked positions in one transaction, delivers the first-buy tokens to your wallet and refunds what it did not spend.
  4. Trade and claim. The coin page buys and sells through Launchpad’s swap router from your wallet, with links to the same pool on Uniswap, GMGN and DexScreener. Graduation is the price crossing the graduation tick: Launchpad’s locker collects the curve pool’s last fees and moves the raise and the unsold supply, by code alone, into the coin’s graduation pool at the same price — a 0.5% pool (plus the holder tax, for a holder-reward coin) whose single full-range position is locked for good, the Robinhood counterpart of Raydium’s migration. The rules switch off; the coin keeps trading, here and everywhere else. A Creator coin’s fee share accrues in Launchpad’s fee escrow under your wallet and you claim it from the coin page; the other options (Holders, Diamond Hands, Earned, Insured) run from a Launchpad wallet made for the coin and pay in the pair asset.

Fees, Raydium’s shape. A Creator, Earned or Insured coin’s curve pool charges 1.75% on every swap: 1.25% for Launchpad (the 1% protocol fee plus the 0.25% a Raydium launch pays Raydium, which has no counterpart here) and a flat 0.5% creator fee — the same flat fee as a Raydium creator-wallet launch; there is no creator-chosen fee. After graduation the pool charges 0.5%: 0.25% for Launchpad and 0.25% for the creator. A Holders or Diamond Hands coin carries no creator fee; in its place the creator picks a holder tax of 1%, 2% or 3% (2% unless chosen) that is part of both pools’ fees — 3.25% / 2.5% at 2% — and funds the holder rewards: 95% of the tax to the holders, 5% to Launchpad. Every number is written on the coin at launch and cannot change.

Transfer rules are available on Robinhood coins too: every catalogue rule except the two app-only rules, the priority-fee cap and the tithe, enforced by Launchpad’s rule engine on every transfer until graduation — a buy from any venue is a transfer out of the pool, a sell a transfer into it, so the rules hold on Uniswap and GMGN exactly as on Launchpad. Gotchi, AI Agent, Market Maker and LP Manager follow on this chain.

From a $20,000 start to graduation

Raydium launches use Raydium LaunchLab's bonding curve, calibrated to a $20,000 starting valuation and an approximately $50,000 graduation valuation using the paired asset's USD price reference when the launch is prepared. The launch's reviewed fundraising target is set in the paired asset. At graduation, Raydium migrates the remaining launch tokens and migration funds into its configured constant-product pool.

With no migration fee, this model allocates approximately 61.257411% of token supply to the curve sale and 38.742589% to migration liquidity. These are token-supply allocations. At the launch-time reference, a fee-free graduation would pair approximately $19,371.29 of the quote asset with the same value of the launched token, giving approximately $38,742.59 in total initial pool liquidity. Total liquidity counts both sides of the pool. The final reviewed amounts account for the selected Raydium configuration, including any migration fee and integer rounding; transfer taxes can also affect amounts arriving in the pool.

The separate liquidity policy then permanently locks 75% of the resulting migration LP tokens and burns 25%. The sale and migration percentages describe coin supply; the 75% / 25% policy describes the LP tokens created at graduation. Fees earned by Launchpad's locked LP position remain protocol revenue.

The $20,000 and approximately $50,000 figures are launch-time USD reference valuations, not USD price guarantees. Changes in SOL, stock tokens, or any other paired asset can change the displayed USD values. Transfer taxes, rounding, migration charges, and subsequent trades can also affect the resulting pool price. Check the actual launch review and confirmed pool configuration.

Existing launches keep their original curve. Updating the site's launch defaults does not rewrite an already-created Raydium curve's sale allocation or fundraising target. An eligible fee or LP-policy upgrade is a separate change; it does not convert an older curve to the new graduation model. Existing $LAUNCHPAD and other live coins retain their original curve parameters.

Earlier PumpSwap launches

The Meteora → PumpSwap mode is closed to new launches. New coins launch on Pump.fun, Raydium or Meteora. Existing coins from this mode keep trading, collecting fees and graduating as described below, and an unfinished paid launch can still be completed from its saved progress.

This mode launched into a Meteora DAMM v2 pool first, targeting a $20,000 starting FDV and approximately $50,000 graduation FDV using the quote asset’s price when the launch is prepared. The quote target is fixed in token units; a changing SOL or quote price can move the displayed USD value.

Meteora’s onchain pool holds the trading reserves before graduation. Buyers add real quote assets as they buy the launched token. At the reviewed endpoint, Launchpad’s controller moves the migration reserves into a PumpSwap pool. The final buy can complete migration in the same transaction; an incomplete endpoint is not a completed graduation. The operator’s funded completion service can cover an eligible remaining gap of up to 0.25 SOL, subject to available funding and verified execution.

Current launches have a 1.25% trading fee before graduation: Meteora takes 20% of that fee, leaving approximately 1% of the trade amount for Launchpad. There is no transfer tax, creator reward allocation or holder rewards in this mode. The current graduation fee is 32.2369073% of the raised quote principal. The remaining quote and reserved launch tokens establish approximately $30,000 of total initial PumpSwap liquidity at the launch-time price reference, including both sides of the pool. Rounding, setup costs and quote-price changes affect actual amounts. Existing launches retain their confirmed configuration.

All PumpSwap LP tokens received by custody are burned. Launchpad does not retain a withdrawable LP position or ongoing LP fee rights from that burned position. PumpSwap’s pool fees apply after graduation. Pair availability depends on exact-mint compatibility with both venues and the configured controller; router support and route availability are not guaranteed. For supported non-SOL launch pairs, the launch flow can convert SOL into the needed quote asset when a verified route is available.

Meteora launches

Choose Meteora for a native Dynamic Bonding Curve (DBC) launch targeting a $20,000 starting FDV and approximately $50,000 graduation FDV, using the quote asset’s USD price when the curve is prepared. Launchpad reserves stay in Meteora’s onchain pool. A completed curve migrates into a Meteora DAMM v2 pool.

Launchpad charges no graduation fee. Meteora applies its own 0.2% liquidity migration charge. With no additional allocations and an unchanged quote/USD price, the model estimates approximately $38,665 of initial pool liquidity at graduation: about $19,333 of quote assets and the same marked value of launched tokens. Account rent, rounding and quote-price changes can change actual results.

Reward baskets

A Meteora launch with holder rewards can name a reward basket: its pair asset plus up to two more verified Meteora pair assets, three assets in all, chosen on the launch page under the holder-reward minimum. The coin itself is unchanged: it trades against its pair asset on one curve and graduates to one permanently locked pool, exactly as any Meteora coin. What changes is what holders receive. (x7pad’s basket mode opens a pool in every basket stock at graduation and pays rewards from each; Launchpad keeps the single pair-asset market and does the splitting on the reward side.)

Every hourly reward round, once at least $100 of the creator fee share is ready in the pair asset, the available amount is split into equal shares, one per basket asset. Each extra share is converted into its asset by the platform through Jupiter from the coin’s own reward wallet, under the same guard as every Launchpad conversion: 1% slippage and at most 2% price impact, the gas wallet paying network fees and any new token-account rent, route costs coming out of that share. Then one holder snapshot is taken and every eligible holder is airdropped every asset, pro-rata by eligible token balance, three recipients per transaction, asset by asset. Each conversion and each payout settles only from its finalized receipt, and each asset keeps its own ledger and its own deferred debts (a recipient a basket asset cannot reach yet is owed that asset and paid in a later round without converting again).

A conversion that has no route or exceeds the impact guard is retried; after three attempts in a round that share is paid in the pair asset instead and the round is recorded with that note on the coin page. When a basket is chosen, the launch page proves a Jupiter route for a $50 slice of the pair asset into each extra asset before anything is prepared, so an asset without a route cannot join. The basket is written into the coin’s public record at launch and cannot change afterwards; a saved draft resumes only with the same basket. The coin page shows the basket, each asset’s distributed and collecting amounts, the round in progress and the last round. Nothing in a reward basket promises any price or return: the extra assets are bought at market with reward money the coin had already earned, and their value moves with their own markets.

Pump.fun baskets. A Pump.fun launch with the Holders option takes the same basket, from the same verified catalogue of up to two extra assets, chosen on the launch page after the pair; each extra asset is checked at the quote (a mint Launchpad can handle, accepted by its swap route, with a fair SOL route for a 0.25 SOL probe purchase — nothing is bought) and the basket is as permanent as on Meteora. The rewards wallet does the buying: when a payout round starts, the holders’ share is split equally, each extra share is bought at market with the coin’s reward money (on a SOL coin directly; on another pair the share is first sold for SOL through the same verified route and then bought) with 1% slippage and at most 2% price impact, three attempts per round before that share is paid in the pair asset instead, and a share below about 0.005 SOL is not bought at all and stays in the pair asset, because network costs would eat it. The round then pays the top 100 holders by minimum balance over the last hour every asset, the extra assets first and the pair asset last, from one holder list; as with Holders rewards on another pair, a holder without a token account for an extra asset needs a share worth about three times that account’s rent, and a smaller share is skipped and spread among the others. Each asset keeps its own ledger on the coin page, with the round in progress, the last round and the skipped shares with their reasons. Diamond Hands, Earned and Insured do not take a basket.

Pair availability is checked by exact mint, token extensions, Meteora permissions, migration support and usable price data. Quote assets with transfer taxes, unsupported hooks or unavailable pricing are not enabled. A listed stock asset is not automatically a launchable quote.

The legacy PumpSwap bonding-curve mode and the Meteora → PumpSwap mode are closed to new launches. Their existing coins and saved trading, fee and graduation operations remain accessible.

Transfer hook rules

A transfer hook is a Token-2022 feature: the coin’s mint names a program that runs on every transfer. On Launchpad the program is Launchpad’s own hook program, and the rules it enforces are chosen by the creator at launch from the 27 rules below, in seven groups, the same rules hooked.fun offers under the same names. Rules are offered on Meteora launches only: Meteora’s Dynamic Bonding Curve accepts hooked mints, while Raydium LaunchLab and Pump.fun do not. The swap that completes the curve revokes the hook (Meteora’s program does this inside that swap, before migration), so every rule ends at graduation; the migration, the leftover withdrawal and every later transfer are plain Token-2022 transfers.

A rule can stop a transfer; it never takes a fee, moves tokens or changes anyone’s balance. The pool, the creator wallet, Launchpad’s fee wallets and the coin’s own vaults are never blocked, so trading fees and graduation always settle. The rules and their numbers are written on chain when the coin is created and cannot be changed by anyone afterwards; the one exception is the allowlist, whose wallets the creator can add and remove. The supply is fixed at creation like every Launchpad coin. The launch page offers the rules only when the host has deployed the hook program; a coin that carries rules shows a Hooked chip on Explore and a Rules card on its page, with the exact sentences the program enforces.

Three routes

Every rule is marked with the route the coin takes while it is on the curve. Trades anywhere (21 rules): the rule reads only the coin’s own on-chain records, so the coin trades through Meteora’s swap path and every app that uses it. Launchpad route only (Allowlist, Holder-gated, Holder vesting and Tithe): the rule needs an account per wallet (an allowlist entry, a gate-token account, a vesting record) or a payment only Launchpad adds (the tithe donation), which Launchpad resolves when it builds the trade and other routers do not, so the coin trades on Launchpad until it graduates and anywhere afterwards. A coin with any Launchpad-route rule is a Launchpad-route coin; the launch page, the coin page and Explore say so. This is the same split hooked.fun calls “Any DEX” and “Hooked route only”. App only (FOMO-only, Pump App-only): the rule allows a pool trade only when the trade’s transaction carries the named app’s mark, which the hook program reads from the instructions sysvar: the app’s co-signing wallet among the signers, a program the app invokes as a top-level instruction, or that program invoked with a named account slot filled (a router’s commission slot). Launchpad never builds such a trade, so Launchpad’s Buy and Sell are off for the coin until it graduates: the coin page says so in their place, names the app, and the server refuses a trade with the same sentence. Sends always work, the creator is exempt, and the rule ends at graduation like every other. An App-only coin is marked on the launch page, the coin page and Explore with the app’s name (“FOMO only”, “Pump App only”). The program is frozen on the launch record with the rule, so a later change of the setting never moves a coin.

Choosing rules and launching

A coin can carry up to four rules. Some pairs cannot be combined because they cap the same thing or decide the same question (for example two sell caps, two per-wallet caps, two trading-hours rules or two oscillators); the launch page greys out a rule that conflicts with one you chose and names the conflict. Percentages are stored on chain as basis points (1% = 100), and shares below 0.01% as parts per million. A hooked coin’s first buy is not part of the creation transaction: it is a separate trade after launch, and the rules apply to it like to any other buy. A hooked launch takes three approvals in your wallet: the curve configuration, the transfer hook setup (the rules, their numbers and the hook accounts) and the coin creation. A blocklist is entered at launch (1 to 200 wallet addresses) and written in the hook setup; a long list takes a further approval per batch of up to 28 wallets inside that stage, and the launch dialog names each one (“Hook setup 2 of 3: blocklist wallets 17–44”). The list is sealed by the first trade: nobody can add or remove a wallet afterwards.

Vesting records. Holder vesting is the only rule that keeps a record per wallet: a small on-chain account that stamps the wallet’s first qualifying buy and totals what it received while vesting applied. Launchpad creates it with the trader’s first qualifying buy (the trader pays its rent); a receipt too small to qualify needs no record. Other apps that route a qualifying buy through the pool have to create the record before the transfer, or it fails with a “wallet not registered” error, which is why Holder vesting is a Launchpad-route rule.

Managing the allowlist. The creator of an allowlisted coin manages the list from the coin page or from My Tokens while signed in with the creator account: the list shows the wallets recorded from changes submitted through Launchpad (the chain is authoritative; an entry changed through another app is not reflected), new wallets are entered one per line (up to 20 per transaction) and existing ones can be removed. Launchpad prepares one unsigned transaction, the creator reviews it and signs it in their wallet, and the list is read again once it confirms. Launchpad never holds the hook authority or the creator’s keys. The blocklist, by contrast, is read-only for everyone once the coin trades; the coin page lists its wallets.

Not offered, and why. Of hooked.fun’s 35 rules, these eight are not offered on Launchpad:

  • Custom hook: Rules written by an AI into an interpreter program. Launchpad’s hook runs fixed, audited-by-reading rules with your numbers; a free-form rule set is not one of them.
  • Pegs: A one-object-per-unit collection with its own art is a different product (NFT-backed units), not a transfer rule.
  • Social trading, OpenSea only: They lock a coin to a trading path Launchpad cannot name on chain. FOMO-only and Pump App-only (an app the host owner names) and Venue-locked and DEX-only are the rules that apply here.

Buy and sell

Use the buy/sell panel on a token page. Raydium trades can route SOL through the paired asset when a verified route is available. Meteora trades use the displayed quote asset directly; keep that asset and enough SOL for network fees in your wallet. Connect the wallet you want to trade with and approve the transaction there. Launchpad does not generate a personal trading wallet for you.

Enter the amount, choose a slippage limit from 0% to 100% in 0.01% increments, and click Buy or Sell. A higher limit permits a worse execution price; 100% sets the minimum output to zero. This setting applies to your token-page trade, not platform buybacks or launch funding. Launchpad checks and prepares the route, then opens your wallet for approval without a separate review screen. Check the transaction in your wallet before approving. Routes depend on available liquidity and provider support. A route can be unavailable, or leave an unused balance of the paired asset, particularly with a high slippage setting. Launchpad trading uses the site's provider connection; you do not need to supply an API key or RPC endpoint.

Pending is not failed. If a request times out after signing, check its saved status and transaction signature before starting another trade. Blockchain confirmation can arrive after a browser request ends.

Creator fees go to the receiving wallet selected for the launch. Claim availability depends on its fee authority and confirmed balance.

For an eligible Launchpad-managed launch, an administrator can redirect creator-fee payouts to a community recipient wallet. Existing transactions must settle before the recipient can change.

Holder rewards

Eligible holders share the available reward balance in the paired asset, based on the distribution snapshot. New Raydium holder-reward launches use a creator-selected 1–3% transfer tax, with 2% selected by default, and no bonding-curve creator fee. After conversion, 95% of tax proceeds funds holder rewards and 5% goes to Launchpad. Existing coins retain their original tax configuration.

Official $LAUNCHPAD exception: when its tax-burn policy is enabled, collected $LAUNCHPAD transfer-tax tokens are burned instead of being sold for SOL rewards. The tax is collected into the coin’s rewards wallet and kept there until the team burns it; it is never sold. Its fixed 2% transfer tax is unchanged. No protocol share is taken from burned tokens. Already owed SOL and rewards funded by the coin’s pool-fee allocation remain payable. The token’s rewards panel shows the collected tax waiting to be burned and confirmed burn totals. Other holder-reward coins retain their existing tax distribution policy.

Paycheck

A Raydium launch, paired with SOL, whose flat 0.50% creator fee is paid out every 15 minutes: 50% to holders by their balance, 35% buys and burns $LAUNCHPAD, 10% to Launchpad and 5% to the creator. There is no transfer tax. Each cycle Launchpad claims the fee vault once it holds at least $1, splits what it claimed, takes a snapshot of the coin’s holders and adds every wallet’s share to its pending amount. A wallet is paid in SOL as soon as its pending amount is worth $0.50 (the creator and Launchpad shares wait for the same line); smaller amounts roll over to the next cycle. Selling or moving tokens before a payout forfeits the pending amount: a wallet that holds less than it did at its last accrual loses what it had pending to the other holders and starts again from its new balance. Buying more never forfeits. The coin page shows what holders are accruing, what has been paid and what sellers forfeited; individual pending amounts are not published.

Existing agent coins

For coins already using agent buyback, available creator fees continue to fund purchases of the launched coin. Purchased tokens are burned after confirmation.

Automatic holder distributions currently use a $100 accumulated-reward minimum, checked against a fresh quote price. This is a distribution budget threshold, not a required holding value. Rounding, eligibility checks, network costs, and reserves affect payouts.

Automatic collection, distributions, and buybacks require the service to be running, the relevant feature to be enabled, and sufficient gas. Enabled holder rewards and agent buybacks retry temporary service errors automatically and continue after a dropped transaction’s expiry has been verified. Their next retry time appears in the rewards panel. An explicit Pause stays in effect until you resume; it does not stop confirmation checks for an already signed transaction. Collection thresholds, spending limits, and available data still apply. Rewards, price increases, and execution times are not guaranteed.

What the platform checks

No real people, no minors, no celebrities, no brand mascots. The tiles cannot express any of them (the age bands start in the twenties), the terms forbid them, and a vision model checks every candidate picture before anything is uploaded: it scores how closely the candidate matches the master portrait (0 to 100; below 70 the picture is generated once more, then the post goes out as words only with a note) and answers whether the picture appears to show a minor, the likeness of a real person, sexual content or text. A picture that fails any of those is deleted and never used. Reference art goes through the same check before it guides a character, and the image provider’s own safety filter can refuse a character, in which case the character reads as failed and the creator may try again with other tiles. Posts are checked for links, mentions, hashtags and price promises before they are queued, and the platform, not the model, chooses the account, the link and the moment.

If X revokes access

When X revokes the connection, or you disconnect the account, posting pauses: queued posts fail and their reservations are refunded, the coin page shows Posting paused with the reason, and the creator sees an alert. Reconnect X on the coin page connects the account again and posting resumes on the next run. Launchpad can also pause an influencer, hide its posts on the coin page or disable the option at any time; generated media that was already posted stays readable on the coin page. Nothing an influencer posts is financial advice.

Dry run

A host in dry-run mode calls neither X nor the image provider: characters and pictures are solid-colour placeholders, posts end as skipped with the note “[dry run] not sent”, every event is labelled [dry run], and the launch page says so above the option.

How the buyback-and-burn program works

  1. Collect revenue. Confirmed protocol earnings are collected into Launchpad's treasury. Unclaimed fees and pending transfers are not yet received revenue; forwarding an already counted fee does not count it a second time.
  2. Convert to SOL. The automation verifies received launch creation fees, curve platform fees, locked-LP earnings and holder-tax revenue shares. It converts supported non-SOL receipts through available market routes. An asset without a usable route stays queued; it is not treated as SOL revenue before conversion confirms.
  3. Allocate 50%. Half of confirmed native SOL revenue and net SOL conversion proceeds is reserved for $LAUNCHPAD purchases. The other half remains available for platform operations, including buyback network costs and a SOL operating reserve. Rounding carries forward so small receipts retain the same allocation over time.
  4. Buy $LAUNCHPAD. The enabled treasury purchases the official $LAUNCHPAD token from its reserved allocation. Purchases use a separate 1% slippage limit and a 2% route price-impact ceiling; visitors' token-page slippage settings do not change these limits.
  5. Burn the purchased tokens. After the purchase confirms, the acquired $LAUNCHPAD is burned onchain, permanently reducing its token supply. A completed burn is established by its confirmed burn transaction.

When enabled, the treasury processes these steps automatically while the service is running and funded. Each conversion, purchase and burn is recorded separately and reconciled before its proceeds can be used again. Small balances accumulate, and assets without a suitable route retry later. A pause stops new automatic transactions while saved signatures continue to be checked. Execution depends on collected revenue, available liquidity, operating limits and transaction costs; there is no fixed execution timetable or token-price guarantee.

The treasury can use a dedicated operator-provided wallet. The operator keeps its backup and direct wallet access, while Launchpad stores encrypted signing access for automation. Initial wallet setup is separate from activation: it does not redirect fees, move funds or start buybacks. Before enabling automation, the administrator verifies the installed wallet address and confirms the change with two-factor authentication.

An existing launch's Agent buyback program is separate: it continues using the launch's own creator fees to buy and burn that launch's token. Burning the 25% migration LP allocation is also separate from burning $LAUNCHPAD tokens.

Common questions

Does every existing coin now graduate at $50,000?

No. The approximately $50,000 launch-time reference applies to new launches using the current model. Already-created curves keep their confirmed parameters. The live USD amount also changes when the paired asset’s price changes; a fee or LP-policy upgrade does not change that curve’s sale allocation or fundraising target.

What should I do if a launch stops at wallet approval?

Open the same coin in your launch page and choose Resume. Launchpad continues from saved progress and checks pending transactions before moving on. Supported wallet safety checks are retained during signing. If approval is rejected, the error applies to that step; earlier confirmed setup transactions remain recorded. Do not create a duplicate launch to retry the same coin.

Why does a chart or holder list say it is refreshing?

These views use provider data and cached observations. During a refresh or outage, Launchpad may show the previous data with a stale label, or an unavailable state. A missing value is not zero. Short chart intervals can have gaps when no trades occurred.

Why is FDV shown in the paired token?

USD values require a fresh market price for the paired asset. When one is unavailable, Launchpad displays the value in that asset instead. More trading volume on the new coin is not a requirement for USD conversion. The original launch reference is not reused as a current market price.

Why is a reward transaction still pending?

Launchpad checks the saved transaction before starting another operation. Once a dropped transaction’s expiry is verified, enabled rewards continue automatically from their saved progress. An expired older tax sale leaves reward balances unchanged and is skipped instead of repeating the old bulk sale. If the provider cannot establish the outcome, Launchpad continues checking the same signature and shows the reason. You can pause future automatic work while these checks continue.

Why does my coin’s address look the way it does?

While Launchpad has addresses in its vanity pool, a new coin’s contract address comes from that pool (for example, one ending in a recognizable pattern); otherwise it is random. On Raydium and Meteora the address is set when you create the launch: sign its first transaction within an hour, or the address may go to another launch and you will be asked to start again (nothing is charged before you sign). On Pump.fun it is set once your funding settles. The coin page shows the address once the coin exists. Each address is used for one coin only, and its key has no control over the coin after the coin is created.

Can a hidden coin still exist on Solana?

Yes. Moderation can hide a launch from Launchpad and restrict use of the site. It cannot reverse its blockchain transactions or remove copies of publicly stored metadata.

How do I tidy up failed launch requests?

Open Launched tokens and choose Archive request on an eligible stopped or failed draft. It moves to Archived tokens in your account and the admin coin list. Hidden coins appear there too. You can restore an archived request; only an administrator can restore a hidden coin to public pages. Transaction history and recovery status remain available. Archiving organizes the list and does not cancel submitted transactions or pause recovery, fee collection, or rewards.

Will failed launches be refunded?

Recovery can return unused setup funds when they remain available and the saved state permits it. Network fees, rent already committed, storage payments, and completed transactions may not be recoverable. Check the launch's recovery status before paying again.

What does the liquidity lock do?

The locked liquidity stays in the pool permanently while its fee rights remain available for collection. Token prices and trading activity still depend on the market.

Do creator fees change with market cap?

The current Raydium policy uses 0% bonding-curve creator fees for taxed holder rewards and a flat 0.5% for creator-wallet and agent-buyback launches. These rates do not change with market cap. Older launches keep their last confirmed rate until upgraded. After graduation, the selected Raydium pool configuration determines the creator-fee rate.

Where can I ask for help?

Start with the official channels this launchpad's operator publishes on this website. Never post a private key, seed phrase, password, or recovery code. If your request includes personal information, ask for a private support channel first.
